THE TWO SYNTHESIS EDGES ARE NOT DRAWN. This is the one place the block was
  not followed, and the reason is arithmetic rather than taste.
  The block proposes enabled edges from Whitworth (1841) to harrison-h4
  (1761) and to leibniz-stepped-reckoner (1673), flagging both as directional
  inversions. The graph reads an enabled edge as "A made B possible" and the
  board measures each one as to.year minus from.year, captioned "N years
  separate X from the work that finally used it". Drawn as written the two
  edges carry lags of -80 and -168 years. Measured against the live graph,
  the archive's mean idea-to-use figure would fall from 41 years to -1.
  The connection the block describes is real. What is not real is the
  causation an enabled edge asserts: Whitworth did not make Harrison's watch
  possible, and he was not answering Leibniz. Neither is reverse direction
  available, since Harrison did not enable Whitworth either.
  So the relation is recorded here in prose and left out of the graph. If it
  should be drawn, the archive needs a synthesis type it does not have, one
  meaning "supplied a precondition an earlier artifact lacked", excluded from
  the lag arithmetic. That is a constitution-level addition and Fai's call.
  Everything else in the block was ingested as given.

THE BLOCK'S FRAMING NEEDS ONE CORRECTION. It states that CARNOT holds two
  plates and that both sit on the line as preconditions rather than as prime
  movers. The line also holds Rudolf Diesel's engine, which is a prime mover
  in the strict sense. The argument for widening the description still
  stands, but on different ground: the line is not short of prime movers, it
  is short of an account of why the measurement work belongs beside them.
  The thesis now reads as three answers to one demand, that a thing work the
  same way in hands other than its maker's. The roster label moves from
  "Prime movers" to "Precision and prime movers", which also settles the open
  question left over from the Harrison ingest, where a marine chronometer was
  sitting under a prime movers heading.

CLAIM 03 IS THE PLATE. Two plates rubbed together become complements, not
  planes, and a convex surface seats perfectly inside its matching concave
  one while both are wrong. Three plates have nowhere to hide, because no
  surface can be concave or convex while agreeing with two other mutually
  agreeing surfaces. Claim 02 states the trap and claim 03 states the escape.

THE TWO CONFLICTS ARE DIFFERENT IN KIND. c-04 is a priority dispute: whether
  Whitworth introduced scraping or refined what Maudslay developed. The block
  notes the second account rests on a single weak source and that no rival
  claimant is named in published copy, and that is recorded with it. c-08 is
  an instrument dispute, and the stronger case is against the archive's own
  published copy: the Science Museum Group holds the machine and says the
  millionth-of-an-inch claim seems exaggerated on thermal grounds, with the
  National Physical Laboratory having shown the claims untrue. Recorded with
  the counter that the ten-thousandth at c-06 is arithmetically derivable
  from the mechanism, twenty threads per inch on a wheel of five hundred
  divisions, and is the figure asserted in published copy. Neither figure is
  printed as settled.

c-10 IS SINGLE SOURCED AND LOAD BEARING, which is an uncomfortable pair. The
  claim that standardisation was driven by maintenance rather than
  manufacture carries the framing of the second half of the plate, and it
  rests on one history of screw thread systems. Flagged in the source note
  rather than quietly leaned on. Worth a second source before it is repeated.

c-12 IS LOGGED AS SYNTHESIS, not as sourced fact. That a standard's function
  is agreement rather than optimality is the plate's conclusion, derived from
  c-11 and c-13, and the source note says so.
