THE PLATE SAYS 1959, THE BOARD PLACES IT AT 1902, and both are right. The
  plate is dated to the public announcement, which is when the process
  existed. The board dates every artifact to its earliest recorded event, and
  the earliest event here is the 1902 patent that described the principle and
  produced nothing. So the pad sits beside Diesel while the plate sits after
  it, and the gap between the two is the story. Worth knowing before someone
  reports it as a bug.

NO SYNTHESIS EDGES DRAWN. All three proposed are resemblances, and two say so
  in their own notes.

  whitworth enabled float-glass. The pairing is the best thing in the block
  and the note describes it exactly: both faced the problem of obtaining a
  flat surface with no flat surface to check against, and they answered it
  from opposite ends, Whitworth removing error after the fact and Pilkington
  inheriting flatness from a liquid that already had it. "Opposite methods"
  is the block's own phrase. Whitworth did not make float glass possible.

  float-glass enabled noyce-integrated-circuit. Same objection, and the node
  does not exist: the artifact is planar-process-ic. This is the second block
  running to name a node that is not in the graph, after united-232-sioux-city
  on the Gimli block.

  leibniz corrected float-glass. corrected is not one of the four synthesis
  types, and the note again describes a parallel: "the same gap closed from
  the other side".

WHAT THE PLATE IS. c-01 sets it up: polished plate ground the high spots down
  until each face reached a common plane, so the deepest deviation on the
  sheet governed how much glass was thrown away, glass that had already been
  paid for and melted. c-02 is the answer: the ribbon takes the level of the
  liquid beneath it. Flatness moves out of finishing and into forming.

CARE TAKEN. c-03 exists to contradict the archive's own earlier draft: the
  ribbon is not untouched. The tin is in continuous contact with its lower
  face, edge rollers may engage the margins, and rollers carry it out of the
  bath. The defensible claim is the absence of grinding and polishing. c-07
  names neither 1902 inventor, because choosing one would settle a priority
  question this plate has not investigated. c-10 separates the first complete
  sheet from a saleable one, three to four years apart. c-11 records that the
  licences were paid, against the common description of the process being
  given away, and notes the later antitrust attention. c-12 declines to give
  a market share figure.
