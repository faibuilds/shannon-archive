=== SHANNON BLOCK (ingestion record) ===
ARTIFACT id: flat-ride | name: The flat ride
  category: rule | origin: United States | LINE: OLLEY L-12, and the line is
  new. Twelfth line, one plate, 1934. Ingested 2026-08-15 (v2.23).

19 claims: 18 verified, 1 unverifiable. The block arrived with 18; claim 18
  was split because it bundled the prior art and Breer's Airflow decision,
  two statements with different sources and different strengths.

THE THREE TAG CORRECTIONS ARRIVED WITH THE SOURCE LINE AND ALL THREE ARE
  APPLIED. They changed what the plate is allowed to say.

  c-flat-ride-07, the naming of the k squared rig, was sent as fact and is
  recorded UNVERIFIABLE. No source states the etymology. The reading is a
  good one, resting on Olley's own dynamic index and on the programme being
  referred to elsewhere as the k squared experiments, but nobody wrote the
  naming down, so it stays in the record and off the site. This is why the
  plate reads 18 verified claims against 19 held, and why the plate never
  calls it the k squared rig in public.

  c-flat-ride-11, static deflection to natural frequency, was sent as fact
  and is recorded as ours, derived, with the derivation in the source note.
  The claim now says in its own text that the relation is derived here and
  that Olley's figure is the deflection, never a frequency. That wording
  exists to satisfy the standing rule below rather than to hedge.

  c-flat-ride-04, the year at Manchester, is Milliken alone. It is attributed
  in the claim text rather than stated flat, and the text says why.

THE BINDING RULE ON THIS PLATE. Olley's 30 per cent is a STATIC DEFLECTION.
  It is not a spring rate and it is not a frequency. c-flat-ride-08 says so
  in its own body, and c-flat-ride-11 says so again from the other side. Any
  future poster, shirt or post drawn from this plate inherits it.

THE VISUAL. Sheet 1 of US 2,092,612, figures 1 and 2, the front suspension
  seen from the front. Public domain, recoloured to the archive's steel on a
  transparent ground, never redrawn. The sheet carries its own header,
  Original Filed Feb. 3, 1934, and Olley's signature, which makes the figure
  its own evidence for c-flat-ride-15. Built with tools/pdf-page.js and
  tools/plate-art.js, both written for this ingest and kept.

VERIFIED AGAINST THE PRIMARY DOCUMENTS, NOT A SEARCH RESULT. Both patents
  were checked on the patent itself. This mattered: a search summary reported
  the Olley filing as 5 February 1934. The block said 3 February and the
  block was right. US 2,092,612 and US 2,054,063 both check out in every
  particular, including Chedru named as the inventor on the patent assigned
  to Dubonnet, and the Belgian priority of 7 January 1933.

THE EDGES: two drawn, one held, one withdrawn.

  DRAWN: flat-ride echoes wright-wind-tunnel, citing c-flat-ride-06. Two
  instruments built to replace guessing with comparison, a tunnel to test
  aerofoils without flying them and a car whose mass could be moved to test
  cars without building them. This is the edge the block asked for after
  withdrawing Carnot, and it is the better one.

  DRAWN: flat-ride echoes crumple-zone, citing c-flat-ride-09. A car arranged
  to disagree with physics on the occupant's behalf, once before the crash
  and once during it.

  HELD: the Petroski edge. The block asks to keep it and anchors it on
  c-flat-ride-16, which is right, but it names a line rather than a plate and
  an edge needs two endpoints. PETROSKI holds six plates and none of them is
  a sealed unit that failed at its seal. The two nearest are tay-bridge-1879
  and hyatt-regency-walkways, and picking between them is an editorial call
  rather than a mechanical one. Held until the plate is named.

  WITHDRAWN: Carnot, on the block's own argument, which is stronger than the
  one that was put to it. The malformed version joins a company closure to a
  person's move rather than an artifact to an artifact. The version that
  would have replaced it, measurement as the precondition, contradicts our
  own ledger: Milliken records that Olley brought Rolls-Royce's existing bump
  rig with him and held that ride could only be settled by subjective
  evaluation. An edge premised on Olley making ride measurable argues against
  the evidence on its own plate.

WHERE THE SOURCE LINE IS THIN, AND IT IS SAID ON THE PLATE. c-flat-ride-18,
  the prior art, is the weakest line here. Decauville 1898, the Lancia Lambda
  from 1922 and the Mercedes-Benz of February 1933 are widely and consistently
  reported without one primary citation each. The claim's source note says
  exactly that, and says why the archive keeps it: it is the reason SHANNON
  never says General Motors was first. Gate 8 requires that cites edges point
  at real sources, not that every claim carries a named primary, so the claim
  ships at that strength with the weakness recorded rather than hidden.

DATES AND THE BOARD. The board dates every artifact by its earliest event.
  The events on this plate begin at the patent filing on 3 February 1934, so
  the pad and the plate agree at 1934. Olley's arrival at Cadillac in
  September 1930 is biography rather than an event of the artifact, and it is
  carried inside c-flat-ride-01 instead. This is deliberate: it keeps the
  board from placing the flat ride four years before the thing existed, the
  way float glass sits at 1902 while its plate reads 1959.

OPEN QUESTIONS, CARRIED HERE AND NOT IN THE GRAPH. Both are unknowns rather
  than conflicts between sources, and unverifiable would have hidden them.

  Did the rig produce the flat-ride rule, or confirm one Olley already
  suspected? Milliken records that he brought Rolls-Royce's bump rig with him
  and held that ride could only be settled subjectively, which makes the k
  squared car an instrument for testing a hypothesis he may have arrived
  with. The sources do not settle the order.

  Is the 30 per cent Olley's own number, or a later convention attributed
  back to him? The front-slower-than-rear rule is his and is documented.
  Whether he stated that ratio, or whether it was systematised afterwards
  from his results, is not resolved in what we hold.

DO NOT PRINT, CARRIED FORWARD.
  That Olley invented independent front suspension, or that General Motors
  did. c-flat-ride-18 exists to prevent it.
  That Olley turned ride into something measurable. He brought an existing
  rig and held that ride had to be judged subjectively.
  Any claim that quieting pitch makes a car less sickening or more bearable.
  The pitch versus bounce claim stays out until the ruling is made.
  That the photographed Dubonnet unit came off a Chevrolet or a Pontiac.
  The delay between front and rear presented as an effect of how the rear was
  tuned. It is geometry, and c-flat-ride-10 says so in its own body.

PEOPLE: three. Maurice Olley contributed, role designed. Gustave Emile
  Chedru and Carl Breer are recorded as people with notes but carry no
  contributed edge to this artifact, because neither contributed to it:
  Chedru designed the unit that was fitted instead of Olley's linkage, and
  Breer declined the whole approach.

STORY: story-flat-ride, "The flat ride". LinkedIn and Facebook both live, so
