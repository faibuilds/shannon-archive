CLAIMS
  - id: c-manchester-28m-01
    text: On 22 August 1985, British Airtours Flight 28M, a Boeing 737-236,
      began its takeoff roll at Manchester bound for Corfu with 131
      passengers and 6 crew.
    status: verified
    source: AAIB 8/88 synopsis (0612 hrs, G-BGJL, 129 passengers plus 2
      infants, runway 24)
  - id: c-manchester-28m-02
    text: At about 125 knots the pilots heard a thump, believed a tire had
      burst, aborted the takeoff cleanly, and turned off the runway.
    status: verified
    source: AAIB 8/88 synopsis and 1.1 (thud at 125 kt; the commander
      thought tyre burst or bird strike; stop ordered in under a second)
  - id: c-manchester-28m-03
    text: A section of the left engine's combustion chamber, a part
      repaired by welding years earlier after a miscommunication over the
      repair manual, ruptured and was thrown out of the engine.
    status: verified
    source: AAIB 8/88 findings 24-47 (No 9 combustor can, 160 mm crack
      fusion-welded November 1983, about 21 months earlier, so "years" in
      the post slightly overstates; subjective manual wording, a deleted
      crack-length limit, and finding 47's inadequate operator-manufacturer
      information exchange)
  - id: c-manchester-28m-04
    text: The ejected section struck a wing fuel tank access panel not
      built to take such an impact; fuel poured out and ignited.
    status: verified
    source: AAIB 8/88 findings 53-55 (panel had roughly one quarter the
      impact strength of the wing skin; neither was required to meet impact
      criteria)
  - id: c-manchester-28m-05
    text: Stopped just off the runway, a light wind of about 7 knots pushed
      the burning fuel against the fuselage.
    status: verified
    source: AAIB 8/88 synopsis, findings 6 and 62 (7 kt from 250 degrees;
      the wind called the principal factor controlling the fire)
  - id: c-manchester-28m-06
    text: The investigators found the crew had acted reasonably on
      everything they knew.
    status: verified
    source: AAIB 8/88 section 2.3, finding 5 ("entirely reasonable based on
      the cues available to them")
  - id: c-manchester-28m-07
    text: The first fire vehicle reached the aircraft in under half a
      minute.
    status: verified
    source: AAIB 8/88 sections 1.1 and 1.14, finding 79 (RIV2 at
      approximately 25 seconds after stopping)
  - id: c-manchester-28m-08
    text: Everyone survived the engine failure itself; the deaths came from
      the cabin, where burning interior materials produced dense toxic
      smoke, carbon monoxide and hydrogen cyanide, filling the cabin within
      minutes.
    status: verified
    source: AAIB 8/88 cause statement, 1.13, findings 63-64 and 99 (48 of
      54 on-board dead had CO and/or HCN above incapacitating levels; fire
      penetrated the cabin within about a minute of stopping)
  - id: c-manchester-28m-09
    text: The overwing hatch weighed 48 pounds and fell inward onto the
      passenger beside it.
    status: verified
    source: AAIB 8/88 section 1.1 (fell pivoting about its lower edge
      across the passenger in seat 10F)
  - id: c-manchester-28m-10
    text: The gap to reach the overwing exit between the exit-row seats was
      27 centimeters, ten and a half inches.
    status: verified
    source: AAIB 8/88 section 1.15, finding 102 (10.5 inches measured
      between row 10 cushions and row 9 seat backs)
  - id: c-manchester-28m-11
    text: Most of those who died were found in the part of the cabin around
      the overwing exit.
    status: verified
    source: AAIB 8/88 section 2.7.2 (approximately 38 of 54 recovered from
      rows 8-12, the overwing exit zone)
  - id: c-manchester-28m-12
    text: The purser fought a jammed door, abandoned it, and had the
      opposite door and slide working about 25 seconds after the aircraft
      stopped. (AS PUBLISHED IN THE POST; the door sides are reversed.)
    status: false
    source: AAIB 8/88 sections 1.1 and 2.7, finding 100. The RIGHT front
      door (R1) jammed from a slide container lid design fault; the purser
      opened the LEFT front door (L1) about 25 seconds after stopping and
      freed R1 about 1 minute 10 seconds after stopping. Correction
      recorded as c-manchester-28m-16. Editorial follow-up flagged to Fai.
  - id: c-manchester-28m-13
    text: That working front door is a large part of why 82 people lived.
    status: verified
    source: AAIB 8/88 sections 1.2 and 1.15, findings 97-98 (82 survived,
      55 died: 53 passengers and 2 rear cabin crew; one survivor died 6
      days later in hospital)
  - id: c-manchester-28m-14
    text: The investigation produced 31 recommendations.
    status: verified
    source: AAIB 8/88 section 4, recommendations 4.1 through 4.31; SKYbrary
  - id: c-manchester-28m-15
    text: The required clear space at overwing exits, and crew checking
      that the people sitting there can open them, trace to this accident.
    status: verified
    source: AAIB 8/88 recommendations 4.10-4.11; CAA access-space rules;
      SKYbrary and Wikipedia on exit-row briefing requirements (the
      "willing and able" wording is the later regulatory formulation)
  - id: c-manchester-28m-16
    text: The jammed forward door was the right (R1), from a slide
      container lid design fault; the purser opened the left front door
      (L1) about 25 seconds after the aircraft stopped and freed R1 about
      1 minute 10 seconds after stopping. (CORRECTION of c-12; not in the
      published post.)
    status: verified
    source: AAIB 8/88 sections 1.1 and 2.7, finding 100
