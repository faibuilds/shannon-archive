ARTIFACT id: container-corner-fitting | name: The container corner fitting
  CARNOT L-10, plate 5 of 5. NEW PLATE, not an unseal. Ingested 2026-08-20

CARD HEAD, AND THE TWO PLACES IT DEPARTS FROM THE BLOCK.
  Year 1970, from the block's own key_year, the month ISO/R 1161 was
  approved. Date reads "1958 to 1971", the block's period, because the
  subject of this plate is an argument and not a moment. That pairing of a
  span in the date with a single year on the board follows
  planar-process-ic, which does the same thing for the same reason.

  Field reads "Standard interface", not the block's "Standardised mechanical
  interface". Every other field on the site is two words: Prime mover,
  Forming process, Marine timekeeper, Precision method. The long form would
  have been the only one that did not fit the card.

THE HOOK IS THE BLOCK'S ONE LINE, MADE EXACT.
  The block offered "agreeing their shape took two years longer than
  agreeing the size of the box". That is 23 months, not two years, so the
  plate carries the two dates instead and lets the reader do the subtraction:
  the size in February 1968, the castings in January 1970. Both are claims,
  C33 and C34, and both name the countries that opposed.

  The hook says eight steel CASTINGS. It never says holes. C05 records that
  the number of apertures is not established from the text held, and the
  standing instruction on this plate is to count castings and never holes.

CLAIMS, 40. Ids c-container-01 to c-container-40.
  36 verified, 3 unverifiable, 1 conflicting. The ledger renders 37, which
  is the verified set plus the conflict, and the panel says so on the first
  line when it opens. That is the archive's existing convention under rule 4,
  not something this plate invented.

  The four that are not plain verified, and what each forbids:
    C05 unverifiable. Aperture count. Never print a number of holes.
    C17 unverifiable. Parent application Ser. No. 682,512, filed 9 Sep 1957,
      referenced by both patents and matched to no issued patent. It would
      be the earliest document in the story if it exists.
    C28 unverifiable. Harlander's 67 per cent. The figure is held but its
      scope is not stated by the source. If ever used, say of the containers
      in service, never of the world's.
    C37 conflicting. Which patent Sea-Land released. Harlander names
      US 3,042,227, but that patent's claim 1 is a shipboard gantry system
      and the fitting is claimed in 3,027,025 and 3,085,707. Say a patent was
      released. Do not say which. It renders with both versions.

SOURCES, 7. Two patents, three ISO documents, Harlander 1982, and the Army
  DTIC report. C26 exists to stop the load figures drifting: they are modern,
  ISO/R 1161 of 1970 carries no load values at all, and any load figure has
  to be dated where it is used.

PEOPLE, 3, AND THE FIRST MERGE DROPPED THEM.
  Tantlinger designed, Harlander opposed, Kirchner filed. The graph carries
  111 people and 127 contributed edges and this block named three, so the
  omission was a gap in the merge and not a decision. Harlander's note says
  in the record that he was a participant on the losing side of several of
  these arguments and not a neutral historian, because most of the standards
  politics on this plate rests on him alone.

  Kirchner's role, filed, is a new value in a vocabulary that already holds
  twenty. No existing role fitted an attorney of record.

EDGES. 40 about, 43 cites, 14 event-of, 3 contributed, 1 covers, 3 echoes.

  The three echoes were re-pointed after the first merge. They had been
  drawn on c-container-01, the load path claim, which is not what any of the
  three parallels is about. They now cite the claim that licenses each one:
    whitworth -> container on C34. Both are interface standards that had to
      be settled before an industry could function, so the claim is the
      settlement itself, opposition recorded.
    float glass -> container on C38. Not a better surface but an inherited
      one; Harlander describes the strength criterion as essentially a
      safety factor of one, which is a refusal to be stronger.
    crucible steel -> container on C24. Behaves the same way twice, which is
      what a material minimum buys.

  E1 to E6 WERE NOT MADE. All six are statements about this plate alone.
  They name no second artifact, and an edge joins artifacts. They are held
  here and are not in the graph:
    E1 to E4 and E6 as written in the block.
    E6 in particular is held as sequence and not cause. A 1965 fitting
      failed on an off centre landing and the modern standard carries an off
      centre landing case. No source connects them and the copy must not
      either.

THE CONSISTENCY THREAD REACHES FOUR, AND THE FIGURE WAS REDRAWN.
  Finding 03 held Whitworth, float glass and crucible steel as a left to
  right chain. A chain was always the wrong picture: an echo carries no
  direction, which is why the renderer gives it no arrowhead, and the three
  already close a triangle on the board. With a fourth joining all three, it
  is drawn as what it is, three instances down the left and the newcomer on
  the right. The title now reads three engineers and a committee, because
  the corner fitting was settled by TC 104 and not by a person.

THE ART, AND THE PHOTOGRAPH THAT WAS NOT USED.
  The block's hero image is ArnoldReinhold's twistlock photograph, CC BY-SA
  4.0. It is not on the plate. All 29 plate art credits in this archive are
  public domain or CC0 and not one is share alike, and a share alike hero
  would put the whole page under a licence obligation the other 29 do not
  carry. The block's own third image is the right one and is public domain:
  US 3,027,025 sheet 11, figures 15 and 16, the same corner separated then
  interlocked. The thesis in two panels, as the block put it.

  GETTING THE SHEET. The attached PDF could not be read. pdf.js hangs on
  these USPTO scans in two different browsers: with the CDN worker getPage
  never resolves, and with disableWorker getPage is instant but render
  hangs. The sheet came instead from Google Patents, whose thumbnail is 82
  pixels wide; the full resolution file is behind meta itemprop="full" on
  each drawing item, and is 2320x3408 at one bit a pixel.

  TRIMMING THE SIGNATURE, WHICH IS THE PART THAT NEEDED CARE. House
  convention on every patent plate is header and signature block trimmed. On
  this sheet no rectangle separates them: the rail's break line slopes up to
  the right and Tantlinger's and Kirchner's signatures sit under it. So the
  trim removed connected components rather than areas. Ink belonging to the
  drawing is one mass of 102,502 pixels that crosses every box; each
  signature word is its own island. A component was removed only if it lay
  wholly inside one of five named boxes: INVENTOR, the two signatures, BY,
  and ATTORNEY. 40 islands went. The drawing was not touched.

  A simpler rule was tried first and was wrong. Removing anything in the
  bottom right corner would have taken the ten hatching strokes inside slot
  125, which sit at x 1174 to 1386 while Kirchner's signature starts at
  x 1359. They overlap in x and are told apart only by row. The report step
  that listed near misses is what caught it.

  Then: crop to the two figures, box average down to 1100 wide so the
  hatching reads as grey rather than moire, and plate-art.js to invert, tint
  to the archive steel and key the paper out. 8.2 per cent of the crop
  carries ink. merch/art-print holds the full 1550x2300 version, so this
  figure never needs tracing for print.

TWO FAULTS FOUND WHILE WIRING THIS PLATE, BOTH FIXED HERE.
  GAUGE_LINES was missing OLLEY. The twelfth line launched at v2.23 and was
  never added to the roster, so the nav, the hero and the gauge all read
  eleven lines live and the footer stamp had been hand corrected to match
  the wrong number. The comment above that roster calls it the single source
  of truth so that no count can drift. The roster itself had drifted.

  Finding edge labels were struck through by their own edges. The label sits
  a fixed distance straight up from the midpoint, which clears a flat edge
  and not a steep one, and this plate's figure is the first with three steep
  ones. Turning the label to the tangent was tried and backed out: it clears
  the curve but on finding 01 two converging edges then crossed each other's
  labels, which is worse. The fix is a halo in the card's own colour, so the
  dashed line passes behind the type. No geometry moved, and every existing
  figure renders exactly as before.
