14 claims: 13 verified, 1 conflicting on the death toll.

THE CONFLICT IS THE INTERESTING ONE. 46 bodies recovered, 59 victims known
  by name, and a Court of Inquiry that counted 56 Dundee tickets at St Fort,
  added season-ticket holders, other destinations and railway staff, and
  believed 74 or 75 were aboard. Archival work on death certificates
  concludes there were no unknown victims and the higher figure came from
  double counting in an early newspaper report. The block's own counter
  stands and is recorded with it: the inquiry did not take its figures from
  the press, it took sworn evidence and did its own arithmetic. No figure is
  printed as settled. It now shows on Where the sources disagree, taking
  that page to 13 open questions across 9 plates.

THE COUNTERPART NODE, created rather than held. The block flagged that the
  forced edge needed a target that did not exist, and offered either
  creating it or holding the edge until a HAMMURABI plate covers the
  Commission. Created, because the graph already models this exact shape:
  tacoma-narrows runs forced -> concept-windtunnel-standard, a bridge
  failure forcing a design practice. concept-wind-pressure-standard-1881 is
  the same move, not a new one. The two now sit beside each other in the
  outcomes lane, both on PETROSKI, both bridges, both standards written
  after the fact. A HAMMURABI plate on the Commission can still come and
  would not conflict.
  The PROPOSED corrects edge to a future Forth Bridge plate is NOT created.
  There is no counterpart artifact and inventing one to hang an edge on
  would be backwards. It is recorded here and can be drawn the day the
  Forth goes up.

FIVE PLATES, FIVE MODES, and the line thesis now says so. Tacoma is the
  failure nobody predicted, Ronan Point the one a spark exposed, the Vasa
  the one everybody watched coming, the Hyatt the one nobody added up, the
  Tay the one nobody was required to look for. The distinction from Tacoma
  is the load-bearing one and it is worth keeping sharp: at Tacoma the
  phenomenon itself was not understood by anyone. At the Tay it was well
  understood and consultants were quoting numbers for it, but no code
  demanded its use. Unpredicted against unrequired.

c-04 REVISED (2026-08-08, v2.02). The claim first read that the wind braces
  "loosened over time and under load". Over time implies a long working life
  and the bridge had under nineteen months in service, so the phrasing quietly
  misdescribed the mechanism. It now reads that the bracing ties worked loose
  under repeated loading, and the source note says plainly that the mechanism
  is repeated loading, from passing trains and from wind, not elapsed
  duration. The other thirteen claims were diffed against the revised block
  and are unchanged.

WHAT THE ARCHIVE IS CAREFUL ABOUT HERE. c-07 carries the block's own
  warning into the record: the collapse of all twelve high-girder towers is
  not evidence against Bouch's defence, because the high girders were a
  continuous run and losing one pier would drag the rest down whatever
  started it. Progressive collapse fits both accounts. c-05 is recorded in
  substance rather than quoted, because the wording could not be traced to
  the primary report and the secondary sources carrying it share identical
  phrasing. c-14, the Redheugh Bridge, is single-sourced and stays in the
  record without going in the post.
  c-03 is the plate: Bouch violated no code, because no code addressed the
  load that destroyed the bridge.

PEOPLE: five, all new. Roles "assessed" for the three inquiry members and
  "maintained" for Noble are new role names and precise. Rothery, Yolland
  and Barlow did not agree with each other, which c-11 records, so giving
  all three the same role is right: they assessed, they did not concur.

EVENTS: two spanned ranges, 1871 to 1878 for construction and the 1880s for

CONNECTIONS, carried and not drawn, neither being claim-cited: to
  HAMMURABI, this is among the clearest cases in the archive of a rule
